Environmental Risk Score & Impact Forecasting

Environmental Risk Score (0–100)

A composite index measuring the localized environmental burden of a data center on its surrounding community.

Score Components

ComponentWeightInputsRationale
Energy Intensity 30% Power draw (MW), grid carbon intensity (gCO₂/kWh), renewable % Data centers can consume as much electricity as a small city. Grid mix determines whether that load drives fossil generation.
Water Stress 25% Water use (GPD), local watershed stress index, cooling type (evaporative vs. air vs. hybrid) Evaporative cooling in a drought-prone basin has outsized community impact vs. the same facility in a water-rich area.
Land & Habitat 20% Campus size (acres), prior land use (forest/farm/brownfield), proximity to protected areas Conversion of forested land or prime farmland is an irreversible loss; brownfield reuse scores better.
Noise & Nuisance 15% Noise at property line (dBA), distance to nearest residence, hours of operation Continuous low-frequency noise from generators and HVAC affects sleep and property values within the impact radius.
Community Burden 10% Tax incentives received, jobs created per MW, local infrastructure strain Public subsidy vs. public benefit — captures whether the community gets a fair return.

Score Calculation

Risk Score = Σ (component_weight × normalize(component_value, min, max))

Each component is normalized to 0–1 using regional baselines (NC-specific where available). A score of 0 = negligible impact; 100 = maximum observed impact in the dataset.

Estimate the geographic area affected by a facility based on its power capacity, cooling infrastructure, and terrain.

Radius Model

Thermal plume (air cooling) 0.2 km +0.05 km per 10 MW Prevailing wind direction, ambient temp, stack height

Visualization Concept

Each facility on the map can display concentric rings showing predicted impact radii by type. Rings are dashed when based on modeled estimates (no measured data) and solid when validated against field measurements.

Future Implementation

  • Monte Carlo simulation for uncertainty bounds on each radius
  • Seasonal variation modeling (summer peak demand vs. winter baseline)
  • Cumulative impact overlay when multiple facilities share a watershed or grid substation
  • Community notification threshold — alert when modeled impact radius reaches populated areas

Scoring Methodology Notes

  • Data requirement: A facility's risk score is only calculated when at least 3 of the 5 impact fields have sourced values. Partial scores are shown with a confidence band.
  • No estimation: Consistent with the app's core principle, missing data fields contribute zero to the score rather than being filled with averages or industry benchmarks.
  • Regional baselines: Normalization uses NC-specific thresholds from EPA, USGS, and Duke Energy service territory data where available.
  • Open methodology: All weights, formulas, and normalization bounds are documented in this tab and versioned in the repository. Community members can propose changes via GitHub Issues.

Integration Architecture (Future)

For a production deployment beyond the GitHub Pages demo, the following architecture would support live data integration:

  • Static layer (current): All 17 facilities with manually-researched impact data stored in localStorage / embedded JSON. Served via GitHub Pages.
  • API layer (future): Serverless functions (AWS Lambda or Cloudflare Workers) that periodically fetch from USGS, EPA, and EIA APIs to update environmental context layers.
  • Satellite pipeline (future): Google Earth Engine scripts that monitor construction activity at known facility coordinates using Sentinel-2 change detection.
  • Community layer: GitHub Issues as a lightweight CMS — structured issue templates for submitting new impact data with required source citations.

About Substation

Substation is an open-source civic transparency tool that tracks the environmental and community impact of data centers in North Carolina.

Why this exists

Data centers are being built across North Carolina faster than communities can evaluate them. When one is proposed nearby, people want to know what it will draw from the area — how much electricity, how much water for cooling, how loud it will be at the property line. Those figures exist but they're buried in county permit filings, utility rate cases, and local news coverage. No database collects them. This app is that database, built in public.

Data provenance

  • Facility locations come from OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL).
  • Impact figures (power draw, water use, building size, campus size, noise) are extracted from publicly available documents: county permit filings, utility commission rate cases, environmental impact assessments, and local news coverage.
  • Every figure links to its source. Each data point shows the verbatim quoted sentence from the source document, a link to that document, and a confidence rating.

Principles

  • No estimates. No averages. No industry benchmarks filling empty fields.
  • An empty field is honest. A plausible guess is a fabrication about a real building in a real neighborhood.
  • Every number must have a quoted source sentence and a URL. If it doesn't, it's not shown.

Contributing

Use the "Add Impact Data" button to contribute researched figures. You'll need the exact quote from a source document and a URL. Contributions are stored locally and shared with other users of the app.
